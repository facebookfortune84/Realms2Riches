## What is a CAS Number?

A CAS Number, short for Chemical Abstracts Service Number, is a unique identifier assigned to a chemical substance. It is a 17-digit alphanumeric code that provides a universal reference point for substances, making it easier to track, identify, and analyze them. The CAS Number system is maintained by the Chemical Abstracts Service (CAS), a division of the American Chemical Society (ACS).

## How Does the CAS Number System Work?

The CAS Number system is based on a hierarchical structure, with each number representing a specific substance. The first 14 digits of the CAS Number represent the substance's molecular structure, while the last three digits represent the substance's physical properties, such as its boiling point or melting point. This unique combination of digits allows for precise identification and tracking of substances.

## Importance of CAS Numbers in Tech News and Analysis

CAS Numbers play a crucial role in the tech industry, particularly in the fields of materials science and chemistry. Here are some ways CAS Numbers are used in tech news and analysis:

* **Materials Science**: CAS Numbers are used to identify and analyze the properties of materials, such as their strength, conductivity, and thermal resistance.
* **Chemical Synthesis**: CAS Numbers are used to track the synthesis of chemicals, ensuring that the correct substances are used and that the reaction is carried out safely.
* **Toxicology**: CAS Numbers are used to identify and analyze the toxicity of substances, helping to ensure the safety of humans and the environment.
* **Regulatory Compliance**: CAS Numbers are used to comply with regulatory requirements, such as labeling and packaging regulations.
